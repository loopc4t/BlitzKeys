About BlitzKeys

Why this typing practice works

What’s in the program?
This practice set includes 2,160 exercises built from 40,310 words, with 5,270 unique words across the material.

BlitzKeys is for English learners who feel stuck at the intermediate level. Maybe you already watch shows, listen to podcasts, and read in English without much trouble, but when it is your turn to write or speak, you freeze up or slow down. That gap between what you understand and what you can produce is exactly what this app trains.

BlitzKeys is not meant to replace listening to podcasts, watching shows and movies, reading books, or writing freely. Those activities give you comprehensible input, the foundation of language learning described by linguist Stephen Krashen. BlitzKeys is meant to sit alongside them, as the piece most self taught learners are missing.

That missing piece is production. Researcher Merrill Swain showed that understanding language is not enough on its own. You need to be pushed to produce it, because that is what exposes the gap between passive knowledge and active use. This is why BlitzKeys asks you to type the whole sentence instead of just picking the missing word. Typing the full sentence forces you to notice what you do not yet control, test your own guess, and correct it right away.

Applied linguist Paul Nation describes a balanced learning routine as needing four parts: understanding input, producing output, studying language directly, and building fluency. Most self directed learners get plenty of the first two through the activities above, but rarely practice fluency on its own. Fluency means taking language you already half know and drilling it until it becomes fast and automatic. That is the one strand BlitzKeys focuses on, using real vocabulary and speed based scoring so known words turn into quick, confident, automatic use.
